Every homeschool group runs on small conversations. A question about a nut
allergy. A change of meeting spot. A thank-you after a field trip. Until now
those lived in five different places. Messages puts them in one.

## One list, newest first

Messages is a single inbox for everything people send you in Famlo. A note from
an organizer, a reply from an organization you wrote to last week, an update
about tomorrow's hike — all of it lands in the same list, sorted by whatever
happened most recently.

Three filters keep it from getting loud: **All**, **Unread**, and **Archived**.
Search runs across every thread you are in, so you can find the message about
the museum deposit without remembering who sent it. Archiving moves a finished
conversation out of the way without deleting it.

## Write to an organization, not a person

When you message an organization, you are writing to the organization — not to
whoever happens to be listed as the owner. Anyone on their team with permission
to answer sees the thread and can reply, so your question does not sit unread
because one person is on holiday.

Organizers get the other half of that: one shared inbox for the whole team, with
a running history of everything a family has asked. No more forwarding
screenshots to a co-leader.

It also means the answer does not disappear when a volunteer steps back at the
end of a term. The thread belongs to the organization, and so does everything in
it.

## The activity is right there

A conversation that started from an activity keeps the activity beside it — when
it runs, where it meets, and what you booked. You do not have to go and look it
up mid-sentence to answer "what time again?"

That context is why an inbox beats a group text. A text thread knows nothing
about the field trip it is about. This one does.

## React without replying

Some messages do not need a reply. Someone confirms a time, or says they will
bring the extra chairs, and "got it" would just be one more thing to read.

React to a message instead. It closes the loop without adding to anyone's
unread count — a small thing that quietly removes a lot of noise from a busy
term.

## Where to find it

Messages lives in the main navigation, with an unread count when something is
waiting, and the bell in the top bar opens the same place. It works the same on
a phone as it does on a laptop: on a small screen you see either the list or the
conversation, and on a wide one you see both.

Start a conversation from the **Message** button on any organization's page, or
from the inbox itself.

One thing worth knowing before you write: the composer's search finds people and
organizations you already have a connection with. It is not a directory of every
family on Famlo, and it is not meant to be — organizations reach their members,
and members reach the organizations they have joined.

## What we left out

We did not build a feed. There is no wall to post to, nothing to scroll, and no
algorithm deciding which family you hear from today.

That was deliberate. The conversations that matter in a co-op are small and
specific — a question, an answer, a plan for Tuesday. A feed makes those compete
with everything else for attention, and the thing most likely to lose is the one
about the nut allergy.

If you want to reach everyone registered for an activity, that is what an
announcement is for. If you want to ask one person one question, that is this.

## Getting started

Nothing to switch on. Open **Messages** from the navigation and it is there,
with any conversations you already have.

If you run an organization, it is worth deciding who answers. Team members can
be given access to every conversation, only the activities they are assigned to,
or none at all — so a teacher can answer questions about their own classes
without being handed the whole roster.

Read more in [Messages](/docs/families/messages), or, if you run an
organization, [Members, roles, and messaging](/docs/organizations/members-and-roles).
